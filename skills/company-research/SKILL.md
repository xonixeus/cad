# Company Research

A short, sourced employer brief that several agents can reuse for the same opportunity.

## When to use

- Watson: interview packs, second opinions on top-tier or strategically ambiguous roles, offer briefs.
- Scribe: cover letters and application answers that need employer-specific content.

Reuse an existing brief on the issue if it is recent (about 30 days) and nothing material changed.

## Before you start

Follow the `research-methods` skill for sources, verification dates and honest reporting. Prefer the employer's own materials, then reputable news.

## Steps

Gather only what the task needs; skip sections that do not apply.

1. **What they do:** products or services, customers, size, ownership (public, private, nonprofit, public sector), locations.
2. **Situation and recent news (last 12 months):** funding, earnings direction, acquisitions, layoffs, leadership changes, major launches or problems. Date every item.
3. **Mission, values and culture:** in the employer's own words, with links. Note any gap between stated values and reported practice, labeled as reported.
4. **The team or function:** where the role sits, who it likely reports to, what the function is known for (from the posting, careers pages, public talks or articles).
5. **How they hire:** published interview process, typical stages, anything the employer asks candidates to prepare.
6. **Pay and benefits signals:** posted ranges for similar roles, pay-transparency data, reputable salary sources. Label ranges with their source and date; never present an estimate as the employer's figure.
7. **Points of connection:** where the client's verified background (cite ledger IDs) or stated values touch the employer's priorities. Mark these as interpretations.

## Output

```
Company brief — <Employer> (prepared <date>)
What they do: …
Recent news: <dated bullets with links>
Mission & culture: … (links)
Team / function: …
Hiring process: …
Pay signals: <range — source, date> …
Points of connection: <interpretation> (IDs)
Gaps: <what could not be verified>
```

Keep it to what someone preparing for this employer would actually use.

## Stop and ask

- Finding the information would require logging in, contacting anyone, or bypassing restrictions.
- Sources conflict on something material (for example, layoffs affecting the team).
