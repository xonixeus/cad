# LinkedIn Content

## When to use

- Scribe: drafting profile sections or posts.
- Watson: advising on positioning, brand and post strategy.

The department never publishes, posts or edits any external profile (`cad:TEAM.md` §6). Everything here is a draft for the client.

## Before you start

1. `client:knowledge/POSITIONING.md`: approved positioning (P1), headline (P2) and summary (P3). If positioning is missing, ask Maestro to involve Watson first.
2. `client:knowledge/CLAIMS_LEDGER.md`: only Verified, Use `Resume` claims for anything stated as fact.
3. For first-person posts: `client:personality_profile/CONTEXT.md` and the client's communication preferences.

## Profile sections

- **Headline:** use the approved headline. Propose changes only as a recommendation with a reason.
- **About:** 150–300 words in the client's first-person voice: what they do, for whom, the outcomes they deliver (Verified claims), what they are looking for if the client wants that public. Front-load the first two lines; they show before "see more".
- **Experience entries:** consistent with the resume's titles and dates; a one-line scope statement plus two to four outcomes. LinkedIn is public, so apply each claim's Limits strictly, and leave out anything the client marked confidential.
- **Skills and featured items:** match the positioning's emphasis; suggest featured items only from materials the client owns.

## Posts

- One clear idea per post, in the client's voice, useful to their intended audience.
- Personal background from `CONTEXT.md` may shape stories, but never becomes a career claim.
- Never name employers, colleagues or clients in a way the client has not approved; never share confidential work.
- Offer two or three hook options and a suggested posting note (audience, timing considerations) without setting a schedule.

## Output

Draft text per section or post, plus a note with claim IDs used, anything left out because it was Pending or confidential, and character counts where LinkedIn has limits. Hand off per `document-handoff`; the client approves before use.

## Stop and ask

- The content would reveal something about a current employer or job search the client may not want public.
- Positioning is missing or outdated.
