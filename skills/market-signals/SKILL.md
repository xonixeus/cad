# Market Signals

Two halves of one loop: Sherlock records signals as it researches; Watson turns them into strategy.

## Recording (Sherlock)

While researching, note signals from **relevant** postings (those in the client's target career paths and seniority), qualified or not:

- recurring required and preferred skills, tools and certifications;
- title variations for the same work;
- posted pay ranges (with the posting link and date);
- shifts you notice (a requirement appearing more often, a title fading).

Post them on the research issue using `templates/signals-log.md`. Count occurrences across postings instead of listing every posting. Never invent counts; if you sampled, say how many postings you read.

## Analysis (Watson)

When preparing a strategy note, quarterly review or development plan:

1. Collect recent signals logs from the client's project issues (state the period covered).
2. Compare recurring requirements with the client's ledger (see the `claims-ledger` skill): already Verified, transferable, Pending, or a true gap.
3. For each recurring true gap, decide whether it is worth closing: how often it appears, whether it blocks Top-tier roles, cost and time to close, fit with the client's direction.
4. Recommend specific development (course, certification, project, portfolio piece) and say why each matters. Separate quick wins from long-term investments.
5. Note title trends that should change search terms or positioning, and pay trends against the client's benchmark.

## Output

- Recording: the signals log on the research issue.
- Analysis: a short section in the strategy note or review: period, top recurring requirements, gaps worth closing (with reasons), search or positioning adjustments.

## Stop and ask

- Too few relevant postings to draw a conclusion (say so instead of generalizing).
- A recommended development item has a significant cost; it goes to the client as a recommendation through Maestro.
